The two words in your title, defined precisely

A logo

A new brand signed to a contract: the platform ($249/mo), its units connected ($46/unit/mo), and brand-level CFO/billing where you attach it. A logo is counted when it is signed and activated. A signature that never goes live is not a logo, it is a lead that died in implementation, and implementation lag is product's accountability to you.

A partnership

What the logo becomes after the sale. The brand names us preferred vendor and hands its franchisees to services for bookkeeping and accounting. That handoff relationship is yours to keep healthy: you are the sales check-in: you coordinate updates back to the brand on accounts sold, new franchisees onboarded, and services attached. You submit that report. You run brand trainings with services so referred franchisees close at better than 50%. You make sure they are happy and keep sending. You do none of the delivery: no demos, no books, no fulfillment. You source, you cultivate, you keep the referral engine warm. Down the road these accounts get an account manager; until then the relationship voice is you.

The 50% close rate on brand-referred franchisees is a shared number: services owns the close, you own the brand's confidence and the training that keeps referral quality high. It is the accountability sales and services hold each other to.

The checkpoint philosophy

The rungs are cumulative and never forfeited, but they are also checkpoints. If December 31 arrives and the company is nowhere near $2M, all four of us are back at the table asking what went wrong, with the data to answer it: how many demos, what close rate, what shipped, what converted. That conversation cuts every direction: sales, product, services, and Taylor. Nobody rides a dead structure for years, and nobody gets ambushed by a review they didn't see coming.

New service lines: the standing door

Every future service line (lending included) is born under the umbrella, and the operator who runs one earns up to 25% of it, with launch gated on proven demand, a funded budget, and founder approval. Your 14% of the umbrella means you own a share of every line that launches whether you run it or not. And if the day comes that lending is real and you want to operate it, that door has a defined shape instead of a maybe.
