Internal · the math behind the equity numbers

The value bridge:
one revenue number, three very different outcomes.

This exists because the same question keeps producing different answers: "what is my stake worth at the $6M gate?" The honest answer depends on two choices, not one: which company we've become (the floor, the thesis, or the bar) and how we financed getting there (bootstrapped, one round, or two). That gives a grid of scenarios, not a single number, and the spread across the grid is not confusion. It is the strategy, priced. Below: every input, every layer, every person, sum-checked so anyone can re-run the arithmetic.

Three scenarios were being quoted as if they were one.

Older documents (the May equity model) quoted figures like "$41M post-A." Those were built on a since-retired forecast ($10M ARR in 2027, the old $4M-2026 target) and the old cap table, and they are superseded by everything below. The floor/thesis/bar framing replaces them.

The gap between A and B is Jared's margin path and the mix shift. The gap between B and C is speed. Nothing else moves the numbers as much as those two things, which is exactly why they are the two things the whole plan manages.

6 · The fundraising overlay

Same outcomes, three funding paths. Dilution hits Rollup, never the umbrella.

Raises happen at Rollup, the product company. That means the umbrella ladders (14% each), Kayden's 3% direct stake, and Jared's 49% of accounting never dilute. Only Rollup-side holdings scale down when we take money. Assumptions, stated: a seed costs existing Rollup holders ~10%; a seed plus a Series A with a pool top-up costs ~27% cumulative (existing stakes scale by ~0.73). Both are standard-shaped rounds, not commitments.

Read the columns honestly and the fundraising question answers itself: two rounds cost Taylor and Kayden roughly a quarter of their Rollup-side paper value. So a raise is only rational if the capital moves us off the floor trajectory and onto the bar: dilution buys speed and probability, or it buys nothing. And the flip side is just as important: because the umbrella never dilutes, nobody at this table should fear the raise. Jared's column barely moves in any world.

One more honest note: a bootstrapped Scenario C is the least likely cell in the grid. Haven and Delightree both raised to hit the paces we call the bar. The realistic best case is "one or two rounds, Scenario C," and those cells still read $17M to $73M per person. The raise is not the enemy of these numbers; slowness is.

7 · The honest caveats

What makes these numbers sayable out loud.

All figures are pre-tax, at full earn of every ladder, and at the stated multiples; the summary matrix above is the bootstrapped case, and section 6 shows exactly what one or two rounds do to each cell. The platform multiple in B and C is earned, not assumed: it requires ~65% blended gross margin and product at roughly half the mix. If either fails, scenario A is what a buyer pays. These are "at these multiples, if we execute" numbers, never promises.

Read the matrix left to right and it is the whole company strategy in one row: margin and mix move you from column A to B. Speed moves you from B to C. That is why the plan obsesses over exactly those two things and nothing else.
